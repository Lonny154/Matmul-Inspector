# Strassen first-action coverage and residual scoring

## Research question

The provenance-beam study preserved alternative children but still could not follow the known seven-term Strassen trajectory. This CPU-only diagnostic separates four possible causes: pair proposal rank, exact `w` rank, complete-action cutoff, and beam admission. It does not change a provider or search policy.

The dominant current exclusion is proposal coverage. Five of seven possible Strassen first terms have pair ranks far outside 128. The other two are generated by bounded top-2 `w`, but both fall outside the final candidate quota. No tested existing provider reaches any of the 5,040 term orderings.

## Candidate pipeline

For each residual, the existing pipeline:

1. Enumerates 80 nonzero ternary `u` vectors and 80 nonzero ternary `v` vectors.
2. Ranks all 6,400 pairs by the exact common-denominator continuous-relaxation bound.
3. Retains 128 pairs.
4. Selects deterministic or exact top-k ternary `w` vectors.
5. Canonicalizes sign-equivalent complete actions.
6. Sorts by exact residual norm and coefficient tie breaks.
7. Returns at most 12 actions before search admission.

The diagnostic uses the repository's row-major factor convention and `{-1,0,1}` domain. It evaluates all four sign-equivalent factorizations of each rank-one tensor. Pair and `w` budgets are treated jointly because the best pair rank and best `w` rank can come from different factorizations.

The full action ranks are among 128,000 sign-canonical nonzero ternary actions. Ties are broken by canonical coefficients. Term 1 shares its score with 5,488 actions. Each other term shares its score with 2,016 actions. Exact ranks therefore describe the implemented deterministic ordering, not a large difference inside a score tie.

## Exact `w` diagnostic

For every sign-equivalent relevant pair, the diagnostic enumerates all 80 legal nonzero `w` vectors and compares them with the separable heap top-k implementation. They agree exactly.

The best initial required `w` ranks are `3, 7, 5, 2, 3, 2, 1` for terms 1 through 7. Terms 1–5 never reach `w` generation at pair width 128. Term 6 needs top-2 `w`; the deterministic baseline rule does not select it. Term 7 is locally optimal for a retained pair. Neither term survives the final top-12 quota provider output.

Output-support diversity does not solve the observed cutoff. Residual and diversity quota providers with 11/1, 10/2, and 8/4 all exclude terms 6 and 7 at the final candidate budget. This is measured behavior for the current diversity rule, not a claim that structural diversity can never help.

## Exhaustive term-order analysis

All `7! = 5,040` orderings were evaluated. Residuals were cached by subsets, reducing the expensive work to 127 nonterminal subset states and 448 subset-to-next-term edges. Every ordering finishes at the exact zero tensor.

No ordering is fully returned by any existing provider configuration:

| Provider | Reachable orderings | First loss: pair selection | First loss: `w` generation | First loss: final budget |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Baseline | 0/5,040 | 3,600 | 720 | 720 |
| Residual quota 11/1, 10/2, or 8/4 | 0/5,040 each | 3,600 | 0 | 1,440 |
| Diversity quota 11/1, 10/2, or 8/4 | 0/5,040 each | 3,600 | 0 | 1,440 |

The beam never becomes the earliest failure for these trajectories because every ordering already loses an action in the provider.

## Offline counterfactual budgets

Joint pair/`w` feasibility has two nondominated measured points:

| Pair width | Top-k `w` | Representative ordering | Largest required complete-action rank |
|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 437 | 3 | `6,1,2,3,4,5,7` | 128 |
| 439 | 2 | `7,1,4,2,5,3,6` | 146 |

Both representative trajectories are fully generated and exactly reconstruct the target. The first ordering's step ranks are `128, 120, 14, 6, 12, 2, 1`. The second ordering's ranks are `146, 102, 15, 13, 4, 5, 1`.

These are offline known-trajectory coverage measurements. They do not establish rank-7 discovery. They also show that increasing only top-k `w` is insufficient. Pair width must rise from 128 to at least 437, and the final complete-action budget must rise far beyond 12 for these observed trajectories.

## Search implications

- Not all seven Strassen terms are available at the initial state. Five fail pair selection and two fail the final quota budget.
- No term ordering avoids a missing action under an existing provider.
- The smallest observed joint alternative-generation budgets are width 437/top-3 or width 439/top-2.
- Pair selection is the most frequent first exclusion: 3,600 of 5,040 orderings for every quota configuration.
- Complete-action ranking is also decisive. It eliminates the remaining 1,440 orderings.
- Greedy residual reduction systematically disadvantages Strassen at the initial state. Every Strassen term has negative immediate reduction, while all eight conventional terms have positive reduction and ranks 1–8.
- A small output-support quota is not enough under the tested ranking. The needed initial actions can rank 128 or 146 even after a wider pair and bounded `w` stage.

The measured facts support a local-objective mismatch as well as insufficient action coverage. Wider pair selection can make a full trajectory representable, but residual-only final ranking still hides its necessary early actions. A different objective, bounded lookahead, or another trajectory-agnostic structural signal may be required. That remains a hypothesis until tested without known-solution bias.

## Reproduction and evidence

```sh
PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/run_tensor_game_w_coverage.py \
  --output artifacts/tensor-game/w-coverage-v1
```

The command refuses to overwrite existing evidence. The archive contains:

- `initial_actions.json`: all seven initial terms, sign-equivalent pair/`w` rankings, scores, costs, and provider stages.
- `trajectory_edges.json`: all 448 subset-to-term diagnostics.
- `orderings.json`: all 5,040 exact orderings and first failures.
- `summary.json`: provider reachability and counterfactual budget frontiers.
- `configuration.json` and `artifact_hashes.json`: environment, command, sizes, and SHA-256 hashes.

The run took about 55.5 seconds of Python CPU time on the recorded environment. No GPU, neural training, or normal search-budget increase was used.

## Recommended next experiment

The smallest justified next change is not a full width-439/top-2/top-146 search. First test a trajectory-agnostic pair selector that mixes the current continuous bound with a small structural quota, then measure whether it recalls the width-437 frontier without consulting Strassen identities. Keep the total returned action budget fixed initially and audit score ranks. If the needed actions still sit near ranks 128–146, test a bounded two-step value or residual objective offline before expanding ordinary search.
